Meet the baker

Meet The Baker Behind The Loaves

✏️ TO BE EDITED: This whole section is placeholder template copy — it'll be rewritten around your real story once you share it.

This started in a kitchen built for a family of four, not a bakery. Early mornings, one oven, and a starter that's been fed every day for three years.

There's no factory, no shortcuts, and no mass production here — just slow fermentation, real ingredients, and loaves shaped by hand the night before they're baked. Every order is for someone whose name I'll know by pickup.

If you've been buying bread that's mostly air and additives, this is the difference good flour, time, and care actually make.

— Hearth & Grain, started in one kitchen

Why it tastes different

Slow Bread. Honest Ingredients. No Shortcuts.

Naturally Leavened

No commercial yeast. A wild starter and a long ferment do the work, which is why the crust crackles and the crumb stays open and tender.

Small-Batch Baking

A dozen loaves at a time, not a hundred. Each one gets shaped, scored, and watched — not run through a line.

Seasonal Ingredients

What's fresh locally shapes the weekly menu — so the focaccia in July tastes nothing like the one in November, on purpose.
