Helping Your Plants Thrive.

Simple guidance can make choosing and caring for plants easier.

Watering Without Overwatering

More houseplants are harmed by too much water than too little. Check the soil before you water, not the calendar.

Read the watering basics
  • Push a finger 2–3 cm into the soil. If it is still damp, wait.
  • When you do water, water thoroughly and let the excess drain away. Empty saucers so roots are not left sitting in water.
  • Use pots with drainage holes. Decorative pots without holes are best used as an outer sleeve.
  • Outdoor pots in sun and breeze dry out much faster than indoor pots.
  • Succulents, aloe and snake plants generally prefer the soil to dry out between waterings.

Understanding Light Requirements

Plant labels describe light in a few common ways. Matching the plant to the spot matters more than almost anything else.

What the light terms mean
  • Bright or direct light: several hours of direct sun, such as an open garden or a sunny balcony.
  • Indirect light: a bright spot near a window, but out of strong midday sun.
  • Shade-tolerant: the plant can cope with less light, but usually grows more slowly. It does not mean no light at all.
  • Pale, stretched growth often means too little light. Scorched or bleached patches can mean too much.
  • When moving a plant into stronger sun, do it gradually over a couple of weeks.

Choosing Plants for Your Space

The best plant is one that suits your light, your space and the time you realistically have.

Questions to ask before choosing
  • How much light does the exact spot get through the day?
  • How big can the plant grow? Some, like monstera and fruit trees, need room.
  • How often can you check on it? Beginners usually do better with forgiving plants.
  • Do you have pets or small children? Some common plants, including monstera, snake plant and aloe, can be harmful if chewed.
  • For outdoor plants near the coast, consider wind and salt exposure.

This is general guidance. Every species has its own needs, so check the care requirements for the specific plant you choose.
